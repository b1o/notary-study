## The general rule

A person who is not a notary may perform notarial functions **only where a law provides for it** ([[znnd:81]]). Every day the office receives documents certified by somebody else: a power of attorney from a mayor, a declaration from a consulate, a deed by a substitute. Before the office relies on such a document, a clerk must check that its author had the power to certify it. An act by a person without the power is null ([[gpk:576]]).

## Overview

| Who | When | What they may do | What they may not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assistant notary | Always, on the instructions of the notary | Everything the notary may do | Real estate deeds, acts recognising ownership, notarial wills and revocations, mortgages ([[znnd:40:1]]) |
| Substitute assistant notary | While the notary is absent, up to 2 years per term | Everything, alone ([[znnd:46:1]]) | Nothing outside the notary's district |
| Another notary of the same district | Under a substitution contract, up to 2 years ([[znnd:47]]) | Everything, with the archive of the substituted notary | — |
| Registration judge ({{съдия по вписванията}}) | When nobody else substitutes the notary, or when the district has no notary ([[znnd:48]], [[znnd:82]]) | Urgent acts; all acts if there is no notary | Acts outside the district ([[zsv:280:2]]) |
| Mayor and municipal officials | Where the settlement has no notary and no district court ([[znnd:83:1]]) | Signatures on unilateral private documents not subject to entry; signature **and** content of a power of attorney; copies | Signatures under [[tz:15:1]], [[tz:129:2]] and [[tz:137:6]] ([[znnd:83:2]]) |
| Bulgarian consul abroad | For documents in Bulgarian ([[znnd:84]]) | Date, content and signatures on private documents not subject to entry; copies; notarial wills of Bulgarian citizens | Deeds for real estate; documents subject to entry |

## The assistant notary in daily work

The assistant notary acts **on the instructions** of the notary and uses the notary's archive and seal. The assistant signs with the addition "помощник" ([[znnd:40:2]]). Offices use a rubber stamp with this word ([[n32:17]]).

> [!warn] Check the type of act
> If an assistant notary (not a substitute) signs a deed of sale of real estate or a mortgage, the act is outside the assistant's powers. Such an act is null. Always check the signature line of deeds from other offices: "помощник-нотариус" without "по заместване" must not appear under a real estate deed.

## Three ways to substitute the notary

### 1. Substitute assistant notary

When the notary is absent or cannot work, a registered substitute acts alone and signs with the addition "по заместване" ([[znnd:46:1]]). In a notarial deed, the names of both the assistant and the notary appear, with the words "по заместване" ([[n32:18:3]]). The substitution is entered in the register of the Notary Chamber ([[znnd:46:7]]); you can check it there.

### 2. Another notary from the same district

A notary may be substituted by another notary **from the same district** under a contract between them ([[znnd:47:1]]). The substitute uses the archive of the substituted notary, puts **their own** signature and seal on the documents and notes the fact of the substitution ([[znnd:47:2]]). The term may not exceed two years from the contract ([[znnd:47:4]]).

### 3. The registration judge

If the notary is absent and nobody substitutes, the notary must inform the registration judge, who takes over **urgent** notarial acts ([[znnd:48:1]]). If the notary did not give access to the archive, the president of the district court may order the entry into the office at the request of a person concerned ([[znnd:48:2]]).

## Registration judges as notaries

Registration judges work at the district courts. Their main job is to order or refuse entries in the property register ([[zsv:280:1]]). They also perform notarial acts:

- in a district **with no notary**, they perform all notarial acts ([[znnd:82:1]]);
- in a district with notaries, only acts on the documents in the archive of the registry office and acts when they substitute a notary ([[znnd:82:2]]).

The fees for notarial acts by a registration judge go to the state budget ([[znnd:86]]).

## Mayors and municipal officials

In a settlement that has **neither a notary nor a district court**, the following officials have limited notarial powers: in a village that is not a municipal centre, the mayor of the village; in a municipal centre, the mayor, the deputy mayor, the secretary of the municipality and the mayor's deputy ({{кметски наместник}}) ([[znnd:83:1]]). They may certify:

- signatures on private documents that are **unilateral** and **not subject to entry**;
- the signature **and** the content of a power of attorney under [[zzd:37]];
- the accuracy of copies and extracts.

They may **not** certify signatures for the transfer of a commercial enterprise, the transfer of shares in a limited liability company and similar acts of the Commerce Act ([[znnd:83:2]]). Fees go to the municipal budget ([[znnd:86]]).

> [!tip] Powers of attorney from a mayor
> Notaries must enter certain powers of attorney in the information system of the Notary Chamber (chapter 20). Municipal officials have limited access to enter powers of attorney for real estate ([[n32:9б]]). In January 2024 the Council of Notaries confirmed, in a position published by the National Association of Municipalities, that powers of attorney for vehicles certified by mayors are valid official documents, even though mayors cannot enter them in the system. Accept them after you check the person and the power.

## Bulgarian consuls abroad

Bulgarian consular officers abroad may, for documents written **in Bulgarian** ([[znnd:84:1]]):

1. certify the date, content and signatures of private documents that are not subject to entry, presented by Bulgarian or foreign citizens;
2. certify the accuracy of copies of documents presented by Bulgarian citizens;
3. make **notarial wills** of Bulgarian citizens.

For a foreign citizen, the consul certifies only if the document is intended to take effect in Bulgaria ([[znnd:84:2]]). A typical case: a Bulgarian who lives in Spain signs a power of attorney to sell an apartment in Sofia at the Bulgarian consulate in Madrid. The consulate certifies the signature and the content at the same time.

## Documents that cross borders

### Bulgarian notarial documents used abroad

Bulgaria applies the Hague Convention that abolishes legalisation. A Bulgarian notarial document gets an **apostille**. For notarial deeds and notarial certifications, the apostille is issued by the **Ministry of Justice**, within two working days ([[plzp:17а:2]], [[plzp:17а:3]]). The apostille certifies the signature and the capacity of the notary, not the content.

For countries outside the convention, documents go through **legalisation** by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and then by the consulate of the country of destination ([[plzp:1:1]], [[plzp:6]], [[plzp:7]]). Signatures of private persons are legalised only if a notary certified them and the Ministry of Justice certified the notary's signature ([[plzp:6]]).

### Foreign documents used in Bulgaria

A foreign document needs, as a rule:

1. an apostille from the issuing country, or legalisation, unless a treaty or an EU rule removes the requirement;
2. a translation into Bulgarian.

When a translation of a foreign document is made in Bulgaria for use in Bulgaria, the **signature of the translator** on the translation is certified by a notary ([[plzp:21а:1]]). This is a common daily act in many offices.

> [!note] EU documents
> Regulation (EU) 2016/1191 removes the apostille for many public documents issued in another EU Member State, for example birth, marriage and death certificates, and offers multilingual standard forms. Check the regulation before you ask an EU citizen for an apostille.
