## Why marriage matters in every deal

A married seller may own the property alone or together with the spouse, depending on the property regime and on how and when the property was acquired. A wrong answer can make the sale challengeable. That is why the parties to every disposition declare their citizenship and marital status ([[znnd:25:8]]) and why the notary checks the population database, which shows marriages and divorces ([[nnbd:3:2]]).

## The three property regimes

The Family Code knows three regimes ([[sk:18:1]]):

1. **Statutory community** ({{законов режим на общност}}). It applies when the spouses did not choose a regime, and always for spouses who are minors or under limited judicial disability ([[sk:18:2]]).
2. **Statutory separation** ({{законов режим на разделност}}): what each spouse acquires during the marriage is personal ([[sk:33:1]]).
3. **Contractual regime**: a marriage contract ({{брачен договор}}).

The regime is registered in a **central electronic register** at the Registry Agency, on a notice from the municipality that keeps the marriage certificate ([[sk:19]]). The register is public. If no regime is registered, a third party who deals with a spouse may rely on the **statutory community** ([[sk:20]]).

## The community of spouses (СИО)

### What is common

Real rights acquired **during the marriage** through **joint contribution** belong to **both spouses**, **regardless of whose name** appears in the deed ([[sk:21:1]]). Contribution can be money, work, care for the children or work in the household ([[sk:21:2]]). Joint contribution is **presumed** until the contrary is proven ([[sk:21:3]]).

### What is personal

| Personal property | Article |
|---|---|
| Rights acquired **before** the marriage | [[sk:22:1]] |
| Rights acquired during the marriage by **inheritance** or **gift** | [[sk:22:1]] |
| Movable things for ordinary personal use, a profession or a craft | [[sk:22:2]] |
| Rights of a sole-trader spouse that form part of the enterprise | [[sk:22:3]] |
| Rights acquired during the marriage **entirely with personal property** (for example, with the price of an apartment owned before the marriage) | [[sk:23:1]] |
| A corresponding part, when acquired partly with personal property (unless that part is insignificant) | [[sk:23:2]] |

> [!example] Typical questions at the desk
> - Elena bought a flat in 2015. She married in 2018. The flat is **her personal property**; she sells it alone, unless it is the family home (see below).
> - Georgi and Vesela married in 2010. In 2020 a flat was bought in Georgi's name only. The flat is **common**; both spouses sign the sale.
> - Vesela inherited a house from her father during the marriage. The house is **her personal property**.
> - Georgi sold a flat he owned before the marriage and used all the money to buy a new flat during the marriage. The new flat is **personal**, but this must be proven. The deed usually states the origin of the money and the other spouse's confirmation.

### Disposal of common property

- The spouses have **equal rights** to common property. During the marriage, neither may dispose of the share that he or she would receive when the community ends ([[sk:24:1]]).
- Either spouse may **manage** common property ([[sk:24:2]]).
- **Disposal** of common property is done **by both spouses together** ([[sk:24:3]]).
- A disposal of a real right in **common real estate** by one spouse alone is **challengeable**. The other spouse may sue within **6 months** of learning of it, and no later than **3 years** after it ([[sk:24:4]]).
- For a common **movable** thing sold for value by one spouse, the buyer is protected if he did not know and could not know that the other spouse did not agree. For gifts and for transfers that need notarially certified signatures (for example a car), the rule for real estate applies ([[sk:24:5]]).

> [!warn] In practice, both spouses sign
> Because a sale by one spouse can be challenged for up to three years, notaries require **both spouses** to sign a sale of common property, or one spouse to sign with a power of attorney from the other with signature and content certified at the same time ([[zzd:37]]). A buyer should never accept "my wife agrees, she is at work".

### The family home

A spouse may dispose of the **family home** that is **his or her personal property** only with the consent of the other spouse, if the spouses have **no other home**. Without consent, the district judge may permit the disposal if it does not harm the minor children and the family ([[sk:26]]). The rule applies also in the regime of separation ([[sk:34]]).

### End of the community

The community ends with the end of the marriage, by a court decision for important reasons, or when the spouses choose separation or sign a marriage contract ([[sk:27]]). On termination, the shares of the spouses are **equal** ([[sk:28]]), but a court may give a larger share in the cases of [[sk:29]]. After a divorce, the former spouses own the formerly common property in **equal ideal shares** until they divide it; each may then dispose of his or her share like any co-owner, with the pre-emption rule of [[zs:33]].

## The marriage contract

- Persons who are about to marry, and spouses during the marriage, may conclude a marriage contract. Only **persons with capacity** may conclude it ([[sk:37]]).
- It contains only **property** arrangements: rights in property acquired before and during the marriage, management and disposal including the family home, costs, consequences of a divorce, maintenance and others ([[sk:38:1]]). It may refer to a statutory regime. It may **not** make premarital property of one spouse common ([[sk:38:2]]). It may **not** contain dispositions in case of death, except clauses on the spouses' shares at the end of an agreed community ([[sk:38:3]]).
- **Form:** the parties sign **personally**, in writing, with notarial certification of the **content and the signatures** ([[sk:39:1]]). No power of attorney.
- If the contract transfers or creates real rights in real estate, a notary in whose **district the property is** certifies it; when the properties lie in several districts, the parties choose a notary in one of them ([[sk:39:2]]).
- Such a contract has a transfer effect and is **entered** in the property register: on the day of the certification if made during the marriage, or, if made before the marriage, on the day the notary receives the **marriage certificate** ([[sk:39:3]], [[znnd:25:5]]).
- The contract takes effect from the marriage, or, if concluded during the marriage, from its date or another date it sets. It cannot affect rights acquired by third parties before it ([[sk:40]]).
- Amendments need the same form ([[sk:41]]).

> [!note] International couples
> For spouses with a foreign element, the EU Regulation 2016/1103 on matrimonial property regimes decides which law applies to their property relations. Before a deal with a foreign spouse, the notary checks which regime applies.

## Children

### Representation

Each parent may **alone** represent a child under 14 and give consent to the acts of a child from 14 to 18, in the child's interest ([[sk:129:1]]). When the interests of a parent and the child conflict, a **special representative** is appointed ([[sk:129:2]]). Example: a parent who inherits together with the child and wants to divide the inheritance with the child.

### The child's property

- Parents manage the child's property in the child's interest, with the care of a good manager ([[sk:130:1]]).
- **Dispositions** of the child's real estate, of movables through a formal transaction (for example a car), of bank deposits and of securities need **permission of the district court** of the child's current address. The court gives permission if the disposal is not against the child's interest ([[sk:130:3]]).
- A **gift**, a **waiver of rights**, a **loan** and **security for somebody else's debt** by a minor are **void**. Only a pledge or a mortgage may exceptionally be permitted by the court, for a need or clear benefit of the child or an extraordinary need of the family ([[sk:130:4]]).

> [!tip] Reading the court permission
> Before the deed, the office checks the court permission: the court, the case number, the date and the **finality** of the decision; the exact property; the price or the minimum price; and any condition, for example that the price must be paid into a bank account in the child's name. The deed must follow the permission exactly.

### Consent for a child to travel abroad

A minor may be refused exit from Bulgaria without the consent of the parents (or guardians, curators) ([[zbld:76]]). If the parents disagree, the district court of the child's current address decides ([[sk:127а]]). The act names no form for the consent. In practice the border authorities ask for a **declaration of consent** ({{декларация-съгласие}}) with a **notarially certified signature**, so a parent who does not travel with the child signs one at a notary. The declaration states:

- the parent's full name, identity number and identity document;
- the child's full name, identity number and passport;
- the countries of travel, or "all countries";
- the period, or "until the child turns 18";
- the accompanying person, or that the child may travel alone or with either parent.

Each parent may sign at any notary in Bulgaria ([[gpk:570:2]]) or at a Bulgarian consulate abroad ([[znnd:84:1]]). The fee is the fee for a signature without material interest: 5 leva (2.56 euro) for the first signature ([[tarifa:5]]).
