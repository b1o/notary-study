## Opening of the inheritance

The inheritance opens at the moment of **death**, at the **last domicile** of the deceased ([[zn:1]]). A person who was not conceived at that moment, or who was born not capable of living, cannot inherit ([[zn:2]]). A person who killed or tried to kill the deceased, falsely accused the deceased of a crime, forced or prevented a will by violence or fraud, or destroyed, hid or altered a will or knowingly used a false one, is **unworthy** and does not inherit ([[zn:3]]), unless the deceased expressly forgave them by an act with notarially certified content or by a will ([[zn:4]]).

## Heirs by law

| Who survives | Shares | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Children (and adopted children) | Equal shares | [[zn:5]] |
| Children and spouse | The spouse gets a share **equal to each child** | [[zn:9:1]] |
| Parents only | Equal shares | [[zn:6]] |
| Spouse and parents (or siblings) | Spouse: **1/2** if the marriage lasted less than 10 years; **2/3** if 10 years or more | [[zn:9:2]] |
| Spouse, parents **and** siblings | Spouse: **1/3** (less than 10 years) or **1/2** (10 years or more) | [[zn:9:2]] |
| Spouse alone | Everything | [[zn:9:3]] |
| Siblings only | Equal shares; half-siblings get half of what full siblings get | [[zn:8:1]], [[zn:8:3]] |
| Siblings and grandparents | Siblings 2/3, grandparents 1/3 | [[zn:8:2]] |
| Nobody up to the 6th degree of collateral relationship | The state; dwellings, studios, garages, housing plots and movable things go to the municipality | [[zn:8:4]], [[zn:11]] |

**Representation.** Descendants of a child who died before the deceased take the child's place without limit of degree. Siblings who died earlier are replaced only by their children or grandchildren. They inherit **per stirpes** ({{по коляно}}) ([[zn:10]]).

> [!example] Calculations
> 1. Ivan dies. He leaves a wife and two children. Each of the three gets **1/3**.
> 2. Maria dies after 12 years of marriage, with no children. She leaves her husband and her mother. The husband gets **2/3**, the mother **1/3**.
> 3. Petar dies unmarried, with no children and no parents. He leaves a brother and a half-sister (same mother only). The full brother gets twice the share of the half-sister: **2/3** and **1/3**.
> 4. Elena, a widow, dies. Her son died before her and left two daughters. Elena also has a living daughter. The living daughter gets **1/2**; the two granddaughters share their father's **1/2**, so **1/4** each.

Remember that inheritance law decides only what belonged to the deceased. If the deceased was married under the community regime, half of the common property belongs to the surviving spouse already; only the other half is inherited ([[sk:28]]).

## Wills

### Who may make a will

Every person who is 18 or older, who is not under full judicial disability for mental illness and who is able to act reasonably may dispose of their property by will ([[zn:13]]). Two or more persons may **not** make a will in the same act ([[zn:15]]).

A disposition of all or a fraction of the estate makes the beneficiary an **heir**; a disposition of a specific asset makes the beneficiary a **legatee** ({{заветник}}) ([[zn:16]]). A legacy of a specific thing is invalid if the testator does not own it at death ([[zn:19]]). A disposition has no effect if the beneficiary dies before the testator ([[zn:20]]), unless the testator named a substitute ([[zn:21]]).

### The two forms

| | Notarial will ({{нотариално завещание}}) | Holographic will ({{саморъчно завещание}}) |
|---|---|---|
| Who writes it | The notary writes the will as the testator states it orally | The testator writes all of it by hand |
| Requirements | Two witnesses; the notary reads it to the testator in their presence and notes the formalities, the place and the date; testator, witnesses and notary sign ([[zn:24]]) | Entirely handwritten, dated, signed after the dispositions ([[zn:25:1]]) |
| If the testator cannot sign | The testator states the reason and the notary notes it before reading ([[zn:24:3]]) | Not possible |
| Safekeeping | In the notary's book of wills | May be deposited with a notary in a sealed envelope, with a protocol on the envelope ([[zn:25:2]]) |
| After death | Effective; the notary issues copies | Must be **announced** by a notary ([[zn:27]]) |

Only the notary personally makes a notarial will; an assistant notary may not ([[znnd:40:1]]). A will that does not meet the form of [[zn:24]] or [[zn:25:1]] is **void** ([[zn:42]]). A will made by a person who could not make a will, or by mistake, violence or fraud is **voidable**; the claim lapses three years after the claimant learns the ground and in any case ten years after the opening of the inheritance ([[zn:43]], [[zn:44]]).

### Announcing a holographic will

The person who holds a holographic will must ask a notary to announce it **as soon as** they learn of the testator's death ([[zn:27:1]]). Any interested person may ask the district judge to set a deadline for presenting the will to the notary ([[zn:27:2]]). The notary announces it by a protocol that describes the condition of the will and its opening, signed by the person who presented it and by the notary; the will is attached to the protocol, countersigned on every page ([[zn:27:3]]). If the will was deposited with a notary, that notary announces it ([[zn:27:4]]). Chapter 26 shows the procedure in the office.

### Revocation

A will can be revoked expressly by a **new will** or by a **notarial deed** in which the testator states the revocation ([[zn:38]]). A later will that does not revoke the earlier one expressly revokes only the incompatible dispositions ([[zn:39]]). A deposited holographic will may be taken back only by the testator personally, with a note in the register signed by the testator, two witnesses and the notary ([[zn:26]]).

## The reserved portion

Descendants, parents and the spouse have a **reserved portion** ({{запазена част}}) that the deceased cannot affect by wills or gifts ([[zn:28]]). The rest is the **freely disposable portion** ({{разполагаема част}}).

| Heirs left | Reserved portion | Freely disposable |
|---|---|---|
| One child (no spouse) | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| Two or more children (no spouse) | 2/3 together | 1/3 |
| Parents only | 1/3 | 2/3 |
| Spouse alone | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| Spouse and parents | Spouse 1/3, parents 1/3 | 1/3 |
| Spouse and one child | 1/3 each | 1/3 |
| Spouse and two children | 1/4 each | 1/4 |
| Spouse and three or more children | Spouse equal to each child | 1/6 |

Source: [[zn:29]].

An heir whose reserved portion is affected may ask for the **reduction** of wills and gifts ([[zn:30]]). To claim reduction against persons who are not legal heirs, the heir must have accepted the inheritance by inventory ([[zn:30:2]]). To calculate it, all gifts (except usual presents) are added back to the estate ([[zn:31]]). Wills are reduced first; gifts are reduced only after that, starting from the **latest** gift ([[zn:32]], [[zn:33]]). Transfers by the recipient of a gift made within one year of the opening of the inheritance can be set aside in some cases ([[zn:37]]).

> [!warn] Why this matters for buyers
> A buyer who buys a house that the seller received as a **gift** from a parent who died recently takes a risk: the other children may ask for reduction. Staff should flag gifts from parents in the history of the property, and the notary explains the risk.

## Acceptance and renunciation

- The inheritance is acquired by **acceptance**, with effect from the opening ([[zn:48]]).
- Acceptance is made by a **written statement to the district judge** of the place where the inheritance opened, entered in a special book; or **tacitly**, by an act that clearly shows the intention to accept, such as selling an inherited flat ([[zn:49]]).
- Any interested person can ask the district judge to set a deadline for the heir to accept or renounce; silence means loss of the right ([[zn:51]]).
- **Renunciation** ({{отказ от наследство}}) is made the same way, by a written statement to the **district judge**, and is entered in the same book ([[zn:52]]). The shares of the others grow ([[zn:53]]).
- Acceptance or renunciation under a condition, with a term or for a part of the inheritance is **invalid** ([[zn:54]]).
- **Acceptance by inventory** ({{приемане по опис}}) limits liability to the value of the estate ([[zn:60:2]]). It is declared in writing to the district judge within **three months** of learning of the opening (extendable by three months) ([[zn:61:1]]). Persons without capacity, the state and public organisations accept only by inventory ([[zn:61:2]]). The heir may not dispose of inherited real estate for **five years**, and of movables for three years, without court permission ([[zn:65]]).
- Creditors of an heir who renounced can ask for the renunciation to be annulled in their favour, within one year of learning of it and at most three years after the renunciation ([[zn:56]]).

> [!note] What the notary does here
> Acceptance and renunciation go to the **district court**, not to the notary. The notary often **certifies the signature** on the statement, especially when the heir sends it by post or through a representative. For minors, the Family Code treats a waiver of rights as void ([[sk:130:4]]), and minors accept an inheritance only by inventory ([[zn:61:2]]). Check the current case law before any renunciation in the name of a child.

## The certificate of heirs

The **certificate of heirs** ({{удостоверение за наследници}}) is issued by the municipality of the deceased's last address from the civil registration records. It lists the legal heirs and their relationship. It does **not** show wills, renunciations or acceptances, and it does not prove ownership. In a sale of inherited property, the notary needs the certificate of heirs, the deed of the deceased, any will, and checks for renunciations at the district court when relevant.

## Partition

- Any heir may ask for partition at any time, even if the deceased forbade it ([[zn:69:1]]).
- A voluntary partition of real estate needs written form with notarially certified signatures ([[zs:35]]); in practice it is often done by a partition contract certified at a notary and entered.
- A partition made **without one of the co-heirs** is **entirely void** ([[zn:75:2]]).
- A co-heir's disposal of **a specific item** of the estate is ineffective if that item does not fall to the co-heir in the partition ([[zn:76]]).
- A partition can be challenged for mistake only if a co-heir is harmed by more than 1/4 of the value of the share, within one year ([[zn:74]]).
- A person may divide property among the future heirs during life, by a gift or by a will, but must include every heir with a reserved portion ([[zn:77]], [[zn:78]]).

## Tax

The surviving spouse and the heirs in the direct line pay **no inheritance tax** ([[zmdt:31:2]]). The heirs file a **declaration** at the municipality of the last domicile within **six months** ([[zmdt:32:1]]).

## Cross-border successions

For deaths since 17 August 2015, the EU Succession Regulation (EU) No 650/2012 applies in Bulgaria: as a rule, the law of the deceased's last **habitual residence** governs the whole succession, and a person may choose the law of their nationality in a will. The European Certificate of Succession issued in another Member State proves the status of heirs in Bulgaria without a special procedure.

> [!reform] Draft law 2026
> The Ministry of Justice draft of August 2026 would enter announced wills in the information system of the Notary Chamber, publish a notice of each announced holographic will on the site of the Chamber, and give one month for objections before copies of the will are issued. Check the State Gazette before you rely on these rules.
