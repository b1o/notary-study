## Where notarial proceedings sit in the Code

Part Six of the Civil Procedure Code regulates the **non-contentious proceedings** ({{охранителни производства}}). In that part, the chapter "General rules" (Art. 530–541) applies to all of them, and a later chapter sets the notarial proceedings (Art. 569–594). The general rules apply to every non-contentious proceeding as far as no special rules exist ([[gpk:530]]). The rules of claim proceedings also apply accordingly, except Art. 207–266 and 303–388 ([[gpk:540]]).

A non-contentious proceeding has no opposing party. A person asks an authority to create or certify a right, and the authority checks the conditions. It does not decide a dispute between two persons. When a dispute appears, it goes to a court by a claim ([[gpk:536:1]], [[gpk:537:2]]). This is the key to most questions in this chapter: the notary certifies, but the court decides who is right.

| Question | General rule (Art. 530–541) | Special rule for notaries (Art. 569–594) |
|---|---|---|
| How does it start? | A written request ([[gpk:531:1]]) | An oral request; a written request only for real estate deeds, declaratory deeds and entries ([[gpk:571]]) |
| Which authority? | The district court of the applicant's permanent address ([[gpk:531:2]]) | The notary of the property's district, or any notary ([[gpk:570]]) |
| What is checked? | The authority checks the conditions of its own motion and may collect evidence ([[gpk:533]]) | Identity, capacity and representative power ([[gpk:578:4]]); ownership and the special requirements ([[gpk:586:1]]) |
| How is a refusal appealed? | Within one week, through the district court ([[gpk:538:1]], [[gpk:538:2]]) | A private appeal before the regional court ([[gpk:577:1]]), within one week ([[gpk:275:1]]) |

The general rules speak of "the court". The notary applies the special rules first. The general rules fill the gaps and explain the nature of the act: for example, the authority may ask the applicant to confirm the facts with a declaration ([[gpk:534]]), and a refused applicant may file the same request again ([[gpk:538:3]]).

## What a notarial act settles, and what it does not

### The act is not appealed; a third person sues

An act that grants the request is **not appealed** ([[gpk:537:1]]). If it affects the rights of a third person, the dispute is settled **by a claim** against the persons who benefit from the act; when the claim succeeds, the court **cancels or changes** the act ([[gpk:537:2]]). The prosecutor may also sue to cancel an act issued in breach of the law ([[gpk:537:3]]).

The Supreme Court of Cassation limited this cancellation in Interpretative Decision No. 3 of 29 November 2012 (case 3/2012, General Assembly of the Civil College): only **declaratory deeds** of ownership are cancelled under Art. 537(2). A deed of a transaction, such as a sale or a gift, is not cancelled. A third person who claims the property sues, and enters the claim so that later buyers are bound by the result ([[zs:114]], [[pv:11]]).

### The evidential force of a declaratory deed

A declaratory deed is an official document ([[gpk:179:1]]). Interpretative Decision No. 11 of 21 March 2013 (case 11/2012, General Assembly of the Civil College) explains what it proves:

- It proves the **acts of the notary**: who issued it, when and where, which documents were presented, and that the notary decided to recognise the ownership. To dispute these facts, a party contests the **authenticity** of the document ([[gpk:193:1]]); the party who contests bears the burden of proof ([[gpk:193:3]]).
- It does **not** prove, with binding force, **who owns** the property. The notary's finding about the owner is a conclusion, not a certified fact.
- It still **legitimises** the holder as the owner. A person who disputes the ownership and has no document of ownership must prove that the holder is not, or is no longer, the owner. When both sides hold notarial deeds, each side proves its own right.

> [!law] Why interpretative decisions matter
> Interpretative decisions of the Supreme Court of Cassation bind the courts, the executive, the local authorities and every body that issues administrative acts ([[zsv:130:2]]). Courts judge notarial acts by them, so notaries follow them too.

## Defects of notarial acts

A notarial act can fail in different ways. The remedy depends on the kind of defect.

| Defect | Example | Effect | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| The notary had no power, or broke a rule listed in Art. 576 | A deed by a notary of another district; a party who did not appear personally; missing signatures | The notarial act is **null** ([[gpk:576]]). A deal that needs notarial form is then void for lack of form ([[zzd:26:2]]) | A new act in the proper form |
| The deal itself is void or voidable | No capacity, threat, a sham price | The notarial form does not cure the deal ([[zzd:26]], [[zzd:27]]) | A claim in court; the claim is entered ([[pv:11]]) |
| A declaratory deed names the wrong owner | Witnesses hid that the possession was not exclusive | Valid until a court decides; then **cancelled** ([[gpk:537:2]]) | A claim against the holder of the deed |
| A wrong date or a false certification | A back-dated endorsement | The authenticity of the document can be contested ([[gpk:193]]); the court sends a false document to the prosecutor ([[gpk:194:2]]) | Criminal and disciplinary liability (chapter 4) |
| A clerical error in a valid act | A wrong digit in an identity number | The act stays valid | Before the end of the signing, a note signed like the act ([[gpk:579:3]]); after it, a correction deed ([[tarifa:1]]) |
| An error of the registry office | The partida shows data that differ from the entered act | A **technical error** ([[pv:33в:1]]) | The person concerned or the notary asks the registry office to correct it ([[pv:33в:2]], [[pv:33в:3]]) |

> [!warn] Null is not the same as voidable
> A null notarial act produces no effect from the start, and anybody can rely on the nullity at any time. A voidable deal produces effect until a court annuls it at the request of the protected party, within the time limit of the law ([[zzd:32]]). When you describe a problem to the notary, say which of the two you suspect, and why.

## The entry as a notarial proceeding

Entries, notes and deletions are notarial proceedings ([[gpk:569]]). They are allowed **only** in the cases that a law expressly provides ([[pv:2]]).

### Who orders the entry, and on whose request

- The **registration judge** of the registry office where the property is orders the entry ([[gpk:570:1]], [[pv:7]], [[zsv:280:1]]).
- Notarial deeds that transfer, create or recognise real rights are entered on the **written request of the notary** who made them. Other acts are entered on the request of the parties, of a notary, or of anybody with an interest ([[pv:8:1]]).
- The judge orders the entry **immediately** after the act is recorded in the incoming register. The act carries the register number and the date ([[pv:9:1]]). The order of the incoming register decides the rank between two acts of the same owner ([[zs:113]]).

### Notes and deletions

| Act | How it is entered, noted or deleted | Article |
|---|---|---|
| A claim against an entered act (termination, annulment, nullity, a final contract) | The claim is entered; the final decision is noted | [[pv:11]], [[pv:12]], [[pv:14]] |
| The end of a court case without a decision | The entry of the claim is deleted on the court act | [[pv:13]] |
| A mortgage | Deleted on the creditor's consent in notarially certified form, or on a final court act; a state creditor needs no certified signature | [[pv:19:1]] |
| A mortgage not renewed for 10 years | Deleted on the request of anybody interested, without other evidence | [[pv:22]] |

### The refusal

The judge refuses by a ruling when the act does not meet the requirements of the law or is not subject to entry ([[pv:32а:1]]). The same rules apply to a refused note or deletion ([[pv:32б]]). The ruling is served against signature ([[pv:32а:4]]). The refusal is appealed with a private appeal before the regional court ([[gpk:577:1]]) within one week ([[gpk:275:1]]). If the appeal succeeds, the entry counts from the moment of the record in the incoming register, so the rank is kept ([[pv:32в]], [[gpk:577:3]]).

Interpretative Decision No. 7 of 25 April 2013 (case 7/2012, General Assembly of the Civil and Commercial Colleges) sets the limits of the judge's check:

- The judge checks only whether the act is **subject to entry**, whether it has the **required form**, and whether it has the **content** that the Regulations on Entries require. The judge does not check the substantive conditions of the deal, unless a law expressly requires it.
- The judge may not give instructions to fix a defective request; the judge refuses.
- For a copy of an announced will, the judge does not check the rights of the testator.
- The **notary may appeal** a refusal of an entry that the notary requested.

> [!tip] What the office does after a refusal
> Read the ruling the same day. If the defect is in the request or a missing document, prepare a new request with the document: a new request gets a new rank. If the refusal is wrong, the notary appeals within one week: a successful appeal keeps the old rank. Tell the parties at once, because the deed gives no effect against third persons until it is entered.

## Evidence before the notary

The notary decides on documents and, for a circumstantial verification, on witnesses. The law sets what counts:

- **Ownership** is proved by the relevant documents ([[gpk:586:2]]). Without documents, ownership can be recognised only by a circumstantial verification ([[gpk:587:2]]).
- The parties declare their citizenship and marital status on the approved form ([[znnd:25:8]]). In an onerous deal with real estate, they also declare in the deed that the price is the payment actually agreed ([[znnd:25:9]]).
- When the evidence is not enough, the notary refuses with reasons ([[gpk:587:3]]). The applicant may present more evidence and file again ([[gpk:538:3]]).

> [!task] Classify the problem
> For each case, name the defect and the remedy. (1) A sale deed for a flat in Plovdiv was issued by a notary in Sofia. (2) A declaratory deed was issued to a nephew, but the neighbours say that his aunt's daughter used the house every summer. (3) The registry office wrote the wrong area in the partida, but the deed shows the right one. (4) The registration judge refused a mortgage deed because the request had no proof of the fee. Answers: (1) null, a new deed in Plovdiv ([[gpk:576]]); (2) a claim of the daughter and the cancellation of the deed ([[gpk:537:2]]); (3) a technical error ([[pv:33в]]); (4) a new request with the proof, or an appeal within one week ([[pv:32а]], [[gpk:577:1]]).
