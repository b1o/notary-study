## Why people use a notarial invitation

A notarial invitation ({{нотариална покана}}) is a written statement of one person to another that a notary serves and certifies. Its value is **proof**: the notary certifies that the statement was communicated to the addressee and when. Typical uses:

| Use | Legal basis |
|---|---|
| A bank declares a loan due early ({{предсрочна изискуемост}}) | The loan contract; [[gpk:417]] for enforcement |
| A co-owner offers a share to the other co-owners before selling to a third person | [[zs:33]] |
| A creditor notifies a debtor about the assignment of a claim | [[zzd:99]] |
| A party gives a written warning before terminating a contract | [[zzd:87]] |
| A party invites the other to sign the final contract at the notary on a set date | [[zzd:19]] |
| A landlord terminates a lease or claims rent; a co-owner claims compensation for use | [[zs:31]] |

By [[gpk:592:2]], the same procedure applies to **any** notices, warnings and replies in civil law relations.

## The procedure in the office

1. **Three identical copies.** The requester presents the invitation in **three identical copies** ([[gpk:592:1]]). Many offices also draft it for the client; the fee for the draft is the fee for an invitation ([[tarifa:18]]).
2. **Check the content.** The text must be lawful ([[gpk:574]]) and must identify the sender, the addressee, the address and the statement.
3. **Register it.** The invitation is entered in the general register ([[n32:7:1]]).
4. **Service.** The notary assigns an employee to serve it at the address under the rules of the Civil Procedure Code for service ([[znnd:50]], [[gpk:37]]–[[gpk:58]]).
5. **Note of service.** The notary notes **on each copy** that the invitation was communicated to the addressee: date, manner, person who received it. One copy goes to the **requester**, one is filed in the **book of invitations** ([[gpk:592:1]], [[znnd:28а:1]]), and one stays with the **addressee**.

The fee is 20 leva (10.23 €) for the invitation; the other copies are charged as copies. When service is done by law ex officio, half the fee is charged ([[tarifa:3]]).

## Serving at an address: the rules

The employee who serves acts as the **server** ({{връчител}}). The rules come from the Civil Procedure Code and apply to notarial service "under the conditions and procedure" of Art. 37–58 ([[znnd:50]]). These articles are written for court papers; the office applies them to invitations accordingly, so the papers stay at the notary office instead of the court.

### Where

The place of service is the home, the holiday house, the workplace, the seat, the place of business, or any other place where the addressee can be found ([[gpk:49]]). Foreigners living in Bulgaria are served at the address declared to the administrative authorities ([[gpk:53]]).

### 1. Personal service

The invitation is handed **personally** to the addressee ([[gpk:45]]). Service on a representative counts as personal service.

### 2. Service through another person

If the addressee is not there, the invitation is handed to **another person who agrees to receive it** ([[gpk:46:1]]):

- any **adult member of the household**, or a person who **lives at the address**;
- a **worker, employee or employer** of the addressee.

The other person signs the receipt with the obligation to give the paper to the addressee ([[gpk:46:2]]). With the receipt by the other person, service on the addressee is deemed made ([[gpk:46:4]]).

### 3. Posting a notice

If the addressee **cannot be found** at the address **for one month** and nobody agrees to receive the paper, the server **posts a notice** on the door or on the mailbox; if there is no access to them, on the entrance door or a visible place near it. If the server has access to the mailbox, the server also puts a notice in it ([[gpk:47:1]]).

That the addressee cannot be found is established by **at least three visits**, with **at least one week** between each visit, and **at least one visit on a non-working day** ([[gpk:47:1]]). This rule does not apply when the server collects data that the addressee does not live at the address, for example from the manager of the condominium or the mayor, and records the source of the data ([[gpk:47:1]]).

The notice states where the papers are left (for notarial service, at the notary office) and that the addressee may collect them **within two weeks** of the posting ([[gpk:47:2]]). The paper is deemed served when that term expires ([[gpk:47:5]]).

### Companies

Merchants and legal entities are served at the **last address in their register** ([[gpk:50:1]]), in their office, to any employee who agrees to receive the paper; the server records the name and position of the recipient ([[gpk:50:3]]). If there is no access or nobody agrees, the server posts one notice under [[gpk:47:1]]; a second notice is not posted ([[gpk:50:4]]).

### Certifying the service

The server certifies with a signature the **date and manner** of service and all related acts, and records the capacity of the recipient after checking an identity document ([[gpk:44:1]]). The recipient signs. A **refusal** to accept is noted on the receipt and certified by the server's signature; the refusal does **not** affect the validity of the service ([[gpk:44:1]]).

If the service had irregularities, it is deemed made when the paper actually reached the addressee ([[gpk:54]]).

> [!warn] Courts check notarial service strictly
> Banks rely on notarial invitations to make a whole loan due. In later court cases, the borrower often argues that the service was not regular. Courts check whether the three visits really happened, at what times, with one week between them, with one on a non-working day, and whether the server recorded the sources of any information. Vague notes such as "nobody at home" without dates and times can cost the client the case.

## A practical guide for the server

**Before you go**

- Check the address in the invitation and on a map; check the building entrance and the apartment number.
- Take: the copies, the receipt forms, notice forms, a pen, your employee identification, and the notary's written assignment.
- Plan the visits so that at least one falls on a Saturday or Sunday and so that a week passes between visits.

**At the door**

- Introduce yourself: name, the notary office, the purpose. Do not discuss the content with neighbours.
- Ask the person who opens for an identity document before you hand over the paper ([[gpk:44:1]]).
- If the addressee is absent, ask whether an adult household member agrees to receive it. Children cannot receive it.
- Never leave the paper with a person who is the **opposing party** of the addressee in the same matter ([[gpk:46:2]]).

**After each visit**, write in the protocol of service:

- the date and exact time;
- what you saw: name on the door, mailbox, lights, neighbours' information;
- who you spoke to and what they said, with the source of any information that the addressee does not live there ([[gpk:47:1]]).

**Safety.** Do not enter homes. Leave if a situation becomes tense. Inform the notary of threats.

The office keeps a record of service in the **expense book** ({{разносна книга}}) ([[n32:4:1]]).

## Declaratory protocols

A **declaratory protocol** ({{констативен протокол}}) certifies that a person **appeared or did not appear** before the notary to perform an act, and whether the persons present **agreed or disagreed** with the act ([[gpk:593]]).

Example: a preliminary contract obliges the parties to sign the final deed at a certain notary on 15 May at 11:00. The buyer comes with the money; the seller does not come. The notary draws up a protocol of the buyer's appearance and readiness and of the seller's non-appearance. The buyer uses it in court to claim double the earnest money ([[zzd:93]]) or the final contract ([[zzd:19]]).

The protocol is made in **two identical copies**, signed by the requester and the notary; one is filed in the special book, the other goes to the requester, charged as a copy ([[gpk:593]]). The fee is 30 leva (15.34 €) ([[tarifa:1]]).

## Safekeeping of documents

The notary may accept documents and papers for **safekeeping** ({{съхранение}}):

- On acceptance, an **acceptance protocol** is drawn up in two identical copies, signed by the requester and the notary. One is entered in a special register; the other goes to the requester ([[gpk:594:1]]).
- On return, a **handover protocol** is signed by the requester, the requester's heirs or a special agent, and entered in the register ([[gpk:594:2]]).
- A case file is opened for each deposit ([[n32:10:1]]); the register of holographic wills and deposits records it ([[n32:9:2]]).

The fee is 30 leva (15.34 €) for documents without a material interest ([[tarifa:1]]) and 50% of item 8, at least 30 leva, for documents with a material interest ([[tarifa:9]]); the return costs 10 leva (5.11 €) ([[tarifa:2]]).

Holographic wills follow the special rules of the Inheritance Act (chapter 26).

## Inquiries and certificates

The notary gives **inquiries** from the notarial books, and certificates about acts performed or about a person ([[gpk:569]]). Only the parties, their successors and their representatives receive information from case files ([[znnd:28а:4]]). The fee is 2 leva (1.02 €) for an oral inquiry and 10 leva (5.11 €) for a certificate ([[tarifa:7]]).

## Protests

The protest of a bill of exchange is also a notarial act served on the debtor. Chapter 14 explains the deadlines and the content ([[tz:501]], [[tz:502]]).
