## The basics

Representation arises by law or by the will of the principal ({{упълномощител}}). The effects of the agent's ({{пълномощник}}) acts arise directly for the principal ([[zzd:36]]). A power of attorney ({{пълномощно}}) is a **unilateral** act of the principal; the rules on contracts apply to it accordingly ([[zzd:44]]).

## Form: match the power to the act

| The power serves | Required form of the power | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A deal that needs a **notarial deed** (sale, gift, mortgage of real estate) | Written, with **signature and content certified at the same time** | [[zzd:37]] |
| A deal that needs **certified signatures** (car sale, partition, lease for entry) | Same form as the deal: certified signature | [[zzd:37]] |
| Transfer of company shares or of an enterprise | Practice follows the deal: signature and content at the same time | [[tz:129:2]], [[tz:15:1]] |
| A partner at the founding of an OOD | Express power with a notarially certified signature | [[tz:114:2]] |
| Requests to the Commercial Register | Express written power ([[ztr:15:2]]); a lawyer needs an express power under the Bar Act ([[ztr:15:1]]) | |
| Information from a notary's case file, by a non-lawyer | Express power with a notarially certified signature | [[znnd:28а:4]] |
| A marriage contract | **No** power: the spouses sign personally | [[sk:39:1]] |
| A will | **No** power: the testator acts personally | [[zn:24]] |
| Banks, the tax agency, the post | No legal form, but institutions require a certified signature in practice | |

The fee for certifying the signature on a power of attorney for rights in real estate is 10 leva (5.11 €) per signature; for re-delegation, double ([[tarifa:5]]). When the content is certified too, the content fee is added ([[tarifa:4]]).

## The scope

The scope of the agent's power towards third parties is **what the principal stated** ([[zzd:39]]). If several agents are named, each may act alone, unless the power says otherwise ([[zzd:39]]). A good power of attorney for a real estate deal states:

- the **principal and the agent** with full identity data;
- the **property** by its cadastral identifier, address and area;
- the **type of deal**: sell, buy, donate, mortgage, divide;
- the **conditions**: minimum price, to whom, how payment is received (into which account);
- the right to **sign declarations**, to receive the price, to pay fees and taxes, to request entries;
- the right to **contract with himself** or with another person he represents, if intended ([[zzd:38]]);
- the right to **re-delegate**, if intended ([[zzd:43]]);
- a **term**, if any.

A power that says "to manage" does not cover a sale. A general power does not cover gifts unless it says so.

> [!reform] Draft law 2026
> The Ministry of Justice draft of August 2026 would require every power of attorney to dispose of real estate to describe the property and the type of deal, and would require the notary to read it aloud, explain its content and legal effect, and note this in the endorsement. The draft would also oblige the notary to check in the information system whether a power has been revoked before relying on it.

## Self-contracting and conflicts

The agent may not contract **with himself** or with another person he also represents, **unless the principal consented** ([[zzd:38]]). If the agent and the other party agree to harm the principal, the contract has no effect for the principal ([[zzd:40]]).

## Acting without power

A person who acted as an agent without power owes damages to the other party if that party was in good faith. The principal may **confirm** the act, in the form required for the power of attorney ([[zzd:42]]). For a sale of real estate, confirmation needs signature and content certified at the same time.

## Re-delegation

The agent may re-delegate only if authorised to do so, or if it became necessary to protect the principal's interests. The re-delegation may be revoked by the principal or by the agent. The agent must inform the principal at once; otherwise the agent answers for the sub-agent's acts as for his own ([[zzd:43]]). The tariff charges double for the signature on a re-delegation ([[tarifa:5]]).

## End of the power

A power of attorney ends ([[zzd:41]]):

- by **revocation** by the principal or **renunciation** by the agent; the principal can always revoke, and a waiver of this right is invalid ([[zzd:38]]);
- by the **death** or **judicial disability** of either side;
- when a legal entity that is principal or agent **ends**.

The end of the power cannot be relied upon against third persons who dealt with the agent **in good faith**, unless the end was subject to entry and was entered ([[zzd:41]]).

### How to revoke in practice

1. The principal signs a **revocation** with a notarially certified signature.
2. The notary enters an extract of the revocation in the **information system** of the Notary Chamber at once, with the number and date of the revoked power and the notary who certified it ([[n32:9а]]).
3. The principal informs the agent, best by a **notarial invitation** (chapter 16), and informs the bank or the other side.
4. The principal asks the agent to return the original.

## The register of powers (information system)

The notary enters **at once** in the information system of the Notary Chamber ([[n32:9а]]):

- extracts of powers with signature and content certified at the same time, and of their revocations;
- extracts of powers for acts before **banks**, and their revocations;
- extracts of powers for **vehicles**, for the **tax agency**, for **investment firms**, for **insurers**, and for **electronic administrative services**.

The extract contains the data of the principal and the agent, a full description of the rights, conditions and term, and a short description of the property if any ([[n32:9а]]). Before relying on a power, the notary checks the system for a revocation. Banks check it too ([[n32:2в]]).

## Powers of attorney from abroad

A principal abroad has three options:

1. **A Bulgarian consul** certifies the signature and content of a power in Bulgarian ([[znnd:84:1]]).
2. **A foreign notary** certifies the power. The form follows the law that governs the act, but compliance with the form of the place where it is signed is enough ([[kmchp:61]]). For real estate in Bulgaria, notaries in practice expect a certification of **both the signature and the content**, or an equivalent act, then an **apostille** (or legalisation) and a translation into Bulgarian with the translator's signature certified ([[plzp:21а:1]]).
3. **A trip to Bulgaria.**

Check that the foreign certification covers the identity of the signatory and the content, not only a copy.

## Common declarations

| Declaration | Typical use | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Citizenship and marital status | Every disposition | On the approved form ([[znnd:25:8]]) |
| Consent for a child to travel | Border control | Certified signature (chapter 10) |
| Origin of funds; political exposure; beneficial owner | Deals under the AML law | Forms under the implementing regulations ([[zmip:66]], [[zmip:59]]) |
| Specimen signature and consent of a manager | Commercial Register | Notarially certified ([[tz:141:3]]) |
| Pre-emption declaration of a co-owner | Sale of a share | Before the notary ([[zs:33]]) |
| Consent of a creditor to delete a mortgage | Registry office | Notarially certified ([[zzd:179]]) |

> [!warn] False declarations
> A person who declares false facts in a written declaration that the law requires before an authority commits a crime under the Criminal Code. The notary explains this before the person signs. A notarial certification of the signature proves who signed; it does not prove that the facts are true ([[gpk:180]]).
