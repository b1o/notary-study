## The notarial proceedings

The Civil Procedure Code regulates notarial procedure in its Chapter 54 ([[gpk:569]]). Notarial proceedings are the proceedings for:

1. legal transactions made by **notarial deed**;
2. **certification of ownership** of real estate; certification of the **date, content or signatures** of private documents; certification of the **accuracy of copies** and extracts;
3. **notarial invitations**, **protests**, certifications of the appearance or non-appearance of persons before the notary;
4. acceptance and return of documents deposited for **safekeeping**;
5. entries, notes and their deletion, where the law provides;
6. **inquiries** in the notarial books;
7. certificates of the existence or absence of encumbrances;
8. other notarial acts provided by law.

Where no special rule exists, the general rules of this chapter apply to every act in the office.

## Which notary is competent

| Act | Competent notary | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Deeds that transfer ownership or create real rights in real estate | The notary in whose **district the property is** | [[gpk:570:1]] |
| Deeds that certify ownership of real estate (declaratory deeds) | The notary in whose district the property is | [[gpk:570:1]] |
| Entries, notes and deletions for real estate | The registration judge of the **registry office where the property is** | [[gpk:570:1]] |
| All other acts, and **wills** | **Any** notary, without regard to the district | [[gpk:570:2]] |

The district of a notary is the district of the district court ([[znnd:3]]). A notary cannot act outside the district ([[gpk:573:1]]). Acts for entry are presented in **two or more identical copies** ([[gpk:570:1]]).

> [!example] Applying the rule
> - A sale of a flat in Varna: only a notary from the Varna district.
> - A power of attorney to sell that flat: any notary in Bulgaria.
> - A marriage contract that transfers a flat in Burgas: a notary in the Burgas district ([[sk:39:2]]).
> - A notarial will that leaves a house in Pleven: any notary.
> - A mortgage on two flats, one in Sofia and one in Plovdiv: a separate mortgage deed at a notary in each district.

## How a proceeding starts

Notarial proceedings start with an **oral request**. A **written request** is required only for a notarial deed that transfers or creates a real right in real estate, for the certification of ownership of real estate, and for entries, notes and deletions ([[gpk:571]]). The written request is filed in the case file ([[n32:11:1]]).

## Parties and participants

- **Parties** are the persons in whose name the act is requested ([[gpk:572]]).
- **Participants** are the persons whose personal statements the notary certifies ([[gpk:572]]). An agent who signs for a principal is a participant; the principal is the party.

The distinction matters for recusal (below), for the identity checks and for the fees: all persons whose request the notary considered owe the fees jointly ([[znnd:87]]).

## Place and time

- Deeds for entry are issued **only in the office** and **only in working hours** ([[gpk:573:2]]).
- Other acts may be performed outside the office and out of hours when good reasons prevent the participants from coming, or when the act must be done at once ([[gpk:573:3]]). Example: certifying the signature of a patient in a hospital on a power of attorney. An additional fee applies: 25% outside the office in working hours or in the office out of hours, 50% outside the office out of hours or outside the town, and double outside the office, out of hours and outside the town ([[tarifa:23]]).

## Legality

No notarial act may be performed about transactions, documents or other acts that are against the **law** or **good morals** ([[gpk:574]]). The notary checks the content, not only the form. A contract that clearly hides a gift as a sale, a power of attorney for an illegal purpose or a declaration with obviously false facts falls under this rule.

## Recusal

The notary may **not** perform an act when a party or a participant is ([[gpk:575:1]]):

- the notary personally;
- the notary's spouse or the person with whom the notary lives in de facto cohabitation;
- the notary's relatives in the ascending and descending line (parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren) without limit;
- collateral relatives up to the **fourth degree** (siblings, uncles and aunts, nephews and nieces, first cousins);
- in-laws up to the **first degree** (parents-in-law, children-in-law);
- persons for whom the notary is guardian or curator, adoptive parent or adopted child, or foster family.

The prohibition also applies when the document contains a **disposition in favour** of any of these persons ([[gpk:575:2]]). Beyond these fixed relations, the notary may decline a request when the notary has an interest in the act or a special relation with the party that raises a well-founded doubt about impartiality ([[znnd:23]]).

> [!tip] What the clerk does
> When a client with the same family name as the notary arrives, ask politely whether they are related. If the answer is yes, inform the notary before you prepare anything. A colleague from another office or the assistant notary under the instructions of another notary cannot solve the problem: the recusal attaches to the act.

## Null notarial acts

A notarial act is **null** when the notary had **no power** to perform it, or when certain rules of procedure were broken ([[gpk:576]]):

| Breach | Article |
|---|---|
| The act is not a notarial act under the law | [[gpk:569]] |
| A real estate deed by a notary of another district | [[gpk:570:1]] |
| An act outside the notary's district | [[gpk:573:1]] |
| An act about a deal against the law or good morals | [[gpk:574]] |
| An act despite a ground for recusal | [[gpk:575]] |
| The participants did not appear **personally** | [[gpk:578:4]] |
| The deed was not read and signed as required | [[gpk:579]] |
| The deed lacks the date and place, the identity data, the content, or the signatures | [[gpk:580]] |
| No interpreter for a participant who does not know Bulgarian | [[gpk:582]] |
| The rules for deaf, mute or illiterate participants were not followed | [[gpk:583]] |
| The persons did not appear personally for a signature certification | [[gpk:589:2]] |

A null act produces no effect. For a sale, this means the buyer did not become the owner. Nullity can be invoked at any time.

## Refusal and appeal

The notary refuses to perform an act when the conditions are not met: a missing document, a doubt about identity or capacity, a recusal, an illegal content. A refusal of a certification can be appealed with a **private appeal** ({{частна жалба}}) before the **regional court** ([[gpk:577:1]]). The term for a private appeal is **one week** from the notification ([[gpk:275:1]]).

- Refusals of entries, notes and deletions are kept in separate books ([[gpk:577:2]]).
- If the court sets aside a refusal of an entry, the entry is deemed made from the moment of the request ([[gpk:577:3]]).
- The general register records refusals too ([[n32:7:1]]).

> [!tip] A refusal in writing
> A client who is refused often asks "on what basis?". The notary explains the reason. When the client wants to appeal, the office gives the refusal in writing with its reasons and the date, so that the client can file the appeal within the week.

## What a notarial act gives the client

- **Proof.** A notarial act is an official document: it proves the statements made before the notary and the acts done by the notary ([[gpk:179:1]]).
- **Enforcement.** A notarial deed, a settlement or another contract with notarially certified signatures, about obligations to pay money or to hand over things, allows the creditor to ask a court for an **order for immediate enforcement**, regardless of the amount ([[gpk:417]], [[gpk:418]]). This is why lenders prefer a notarial form. Since 1 July 2025 the creditor presents the original of the notarial document: with a paper application, or within seven days after an electronic application ([[gpk:417:2]], [[gpk:417:3]]).
- **Priority.** A deed entered in the property register can be relied upon against later acquirers (chapter 9).

## Fees

Notarial fees are due **when the act is performed**; the fee for a circumstantial verification is due when the request is made ([[znnd:88:1]]). The notary may ask for an advance ([[znnd:88:2]]). The office issues a bill ([[znnd:89]]). Chapter 28 explains the calculation.
