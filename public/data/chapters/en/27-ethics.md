## The ethical frame

The notary must observe **professional ethics** and protect the **prestige** of the profession under the statute of the Notary Chamber ([[znnd:34:1]]). The notary must also introduce, train and guide the assistant notary and the employees ([[znnd:34:2]]). The statute of the Notary Chamber gives the detailed rules. On taking office, every notary swears to perform the duties honestly, conscientiously and impartially and to keep professional secrecy ([[znnd:15:1]]).

For the whole office this means:

| Principle | In practice | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Legality** | No act about deals or documents against the law or good morals | [[gpk:574]] |
| **Impartiality** | The office serves both parties; it does not take sides | [[znnd:24]] |
| **Independence** | Only the law governs the notary's decisions; not the client, not the agent, not the bank | [[znnd:17:1]] |
| **Care and explanation** | Clarify the will and the facts; explain the legal consequences clearly; avoid omissions and delays | [[znnd:25:1]] |
| **Secrecy** | Nothing learned at work leaves the office | [[znnd:26]], [[znnd:49]] |
| **Personal performance** | The notary acts personally; staff never certify | [[znnd:2:3]] |
| **No conflicts** | The notary steps aside for relatives and interests | [[gpk:575]], [[znnd:23]] |

## Client service

### At the reception desk

- Greet, listen, and find out **what the client wants to achieve**, not only which document they ask for. A client who asks for "a power of attorney" may need a gift deed, or a consent for a child to travel.
- Explain which documents to bring and why. Give a written list.
- Tell the client the fees and costs before work starts. The notary may ask for part of the fee in advance ([[znnd:88:2]]), and the bill shows the legal basis of each fee ([[znnd:89:2]]).
- Do **not** give legal opinions on the client's rights. Pass legal questions to the notary or the assistant notary. Oral consultations by the notary in connection with a charged act are free ([[znnd:93:2]]).
- Do not recommend specific estate agents, lawyers or banks for a commission. Do not accept gifts from clients or agents.

### Explaining consequences

The duty to explain belongs to the notary ([[znnd:25:1]]), but staff prepare the ground. Typical explanations:

- A **gift** passes ownership at once and can be revoked only for serious reasons ([[zzd:225]], [[zzd:227]]).
- A **sale with a reserved right of use** makes the buyer the owner now; the seller keeps only the right to use the property and receive its fruits, and cannot transfer this right ([[zs:56]]).
- A **care contract** passes ownership now; if the care stops, only a court can end the contract ([[zzd:87]]).
- A **general power of attorney** lets the agent act as the client. The client can revoke it at any time ([[zzd:38]]).

## Vulnerable clients

Some clients cannot protect their interests well: very old people, people who are ill, people who do not speak Bulgarian, people with low literacy, people in grief, people in debt.

- **Meet alone.** The notary meets the person alone, unless the person wants others present ([[znnd:20]]). Ask relatives to wait outside.
- **Plain language.** Use short sentences. Ask the client to explain in their own words what they are doing.
- **Interpreter.** The notary appoints an interpreter when a participant does not know Bulgarian and the notary does not know the participant's language ([[gpk:582]]). A person who gains from the act, a relative of the notary and office staff cannot be interpreters ([[gpk:584]]).
- **Capacity.** If the person cannot understand or control their acts, the contract is voidable ([[zzd:31]]). Postpone the act and, if appropriate, ask for a medical opinion.
- **Time.** Offer time to think. A deed that must happen "today" for an old person is a warning sign.

## Fraud: the warning signs

Property fraud in Bulgaria often uses the notary office to give a false deal the force of an official document. Typical schemes and signs:

| Scheme | Warning signs |
|---|---|
| **Impostor owner** with a forged or borrowed identity card | Face does not match well; card recently issued or reported lost; the register shows a different photo or document number ([[znnd:25:3]]); the "owner" knows little about the property; hurried sale below market price |
| **Forged power of attorney** | Power from a far-away notary or abroad; not found in the information system; recently certified; wide powers; the principal cannot be reached ([[n32:9а]]) |
| **Revoked power** used anyway | Revocation in the information system ([[n32:9а]]); the agent avoids contact with the principal |
| **Pressure on an elderly owner** | A relative or "friend" answers the questions; the owner is confused; transfer as a gift or care contract to a non-relative; a new power of attorney just before the deal |
| **False witnesses** in a circumstantial verification | Witnesses who are not neighbours; vague or identical statements; a property known to belong to others ([[gpk:587:2]]) |
| **Forged holographic will** | A will presented by a beneficiary right after the death; handwriting doubts; heirs excluded without explanation |
| **Sham price** | Price far from market; cash; parties ask to write a lower price ([[znnd:25:9]]) |
| **Money laundering** | Third-party payments; complex company structures; quick resales (chapter 23) |

## What to do when something is wrong

1. **Stop** the process calmly. Do not accuse the client.
2. **Inform the notary** at once, discreetly.
3. **Check** more: the registers, the information system, the earlier documents and signatures, the Commercial Register.
4. **Ask questions** that a real owner can answer.
5. **Refuse** if doubts remain. The notary does not perform an act against the law ([[gpk:574]]). A refusal can be appealed to the regional court within one week ([[gpk:577:1]], [[gpk:275:1]]). Give the refusal in writing with the reasons, so the client can appeal.
6. **Document** everything in the case file: who came, what documents, what was checked, why the act was refused.
7. **Report** a crime to the prosecutor or the police, and a suspicion of money laundering to the Financial Intelligence Directorate, without tipping off ([[zmip:72]], [[zmip:80]]).

> [!warn] The cost of one mistake
> A notarial act performed for an impostor makes the real owner lose time and money in court, and the notary is liable up to the material interest ([[znnd:73:1]]). A refusal costs one unhappy client. When in doubt, refuse.

## Complaints and difficult situations

- **An angry client.** Stay calm, listen, explain the legal reason in plain words, offer the next step (a missing document, a court permission). Never argue about the law at the desk.
- **A client who demands a "small favour"** (back-dating, a missing signature "later", a certification without the person): say no: a certification without the person is null ([[gpk:576]]), a deed must carry its true date ([[gpk:580]]), and a false date in an official document is a crime for the notary.
- **A complaint about the notary.** Clients may complain to the Council of Notaries or to the Minister of Justice; both can start disciplinary proceedings or inspections ([[znnd:77:1]], [[znnd:80б:2]]). Pass every written complaint to the notary at once.
- **Media and social networks.** Do not discuss clients, deals or famous visitors, even without names.
